Cavern 3 Simulator

One waste-disposal cavern, run forward day by day. Every cubic metre received is tracked into the phase it ends up in, and the capacity ledger is a consequence of the physics rather than an assumption laid on top of it.

Physics mirrors the reconciliation engine — physics.py, density.py and models.py in the cavern codebase. Every constant is that codebase's own default, listed with its line number in The model below, and the port is checked against it day by day by sim/verify_against_reference_engine.py. The settling layer is ours — the codebase has no settling model, so the settled / pending split the Capacity Ledger sells is derived here from Stokes' law and marked as such wherever it appears. Loads are simulated on a fixed seed. No Pure operating data is used anywhere on this page.

The model, in full

nothing here is hidden and nothing here is fitted

01  The closure identity

Vvoid − Vbrine − Vgrains − Vbed − Voil = 0

This is the whole engine. Every step ends by driving this residual to zero, and the only free variable left to do it with is the volume of brine displaced out of the annulus. Nothing is solved by adjusting a phase to make the books balance.

The codebase writes it without the suspended-grain term, because it has no suspension: physics.py phase_volumes(). Switch settling off here and the two are identical, which is what the verification script checks.

02  Brine density

ρL = 998.2 + 764.4·wNaCl + 350·wother − 0.3·(T − 20)

Straight from density.py, coefficients unchanged. At saturation wNaCl = 0.264 it returns 1,200.0 kg/m³ carrying 316.8 kg of NaCl per cubic metre. The model's own docstring calls it an engineering placeholder, not a site-calibrated equation of state, and it refuses any wNaCl above 0.28.

03  Where the wash ratio comes from

1 m³ halite → 2165 × 0.98 = 2,121.7 kg NaCl
brine = 2121.7 / 316.8 = 6.697 m³
fresh = 6.697 × 0.8848 = 5.926 m³

So 5.93 m³ of freshwater per m³ of void is the floor, and it is a floor because it assumes every litre leaves fully saturated. At 100 per cent pure halite it is 6.05. Any figure below that is not a tight operation, it is an arithmetic error.

04  Insolubles refill the void as it is cut

mins = 2165 × (1 − 0.98) = 43.3 kg / m³ rock
Vbulk = 43.3 / (2650 × (1 − φs))

At the fresh porosity used here, φs = 0.40, that is 0.0272 m³, so 2.72 per cent of every cubic metre mined refills itself immediately. Compacted to the codebase's asymptote of 0.25 it settles back to 2.18 per cent. This is one of the two sinks in the Phase 00 material, and the codebase's own salt-purity default is what sets its size.

05  Sediment volume and compaction

Vbed = Ms / (ρgrain(1 − φs))
dφs/dt = −kc(φs − φs,∞)
φs(t) = φs,∞ + (φs,0 − φs,∞)e−kct

Both from physics.py sediment_volume_m3() and compacted_porosity(). With kc = 0.001/day the bed has a 693-day half-life toward its asymptote, so a cavern recovers a few per cent of its bed volume over the first few years without anything being removed.

06  Settling — the part the codebase does not have

v = (ρp − ρf) · g · d² / (18 µ)

Stokes, with ρp = 2650 kg/m³ from the codebase and µ interpolated linearly from 1.002 mPa·s at fresh water to 1.99 mPa·s at saturation. That interpolation is ours and is the one piece of physics on this page with no counterpart in either artifact.

It reproduces both claims the proposal makes without showing the arithmetic: solids fall 2.26× slower in saturated brine than in water (the deck says “two to three times”), and at d₅₀ = 20 µm a load clears a 119 m column in 8.7 days (the deck says “clearing over the next 9 days”).

07  Why settling costs capacity twice

suspended V = Ms / ρgrain
settled V = Ms / (ρgrain(1 − φs))
gain = φs / (1 − φs) = 33.3% at φs = 0.25

A grain in suspension displaces its own volume. The same grain in the bed drags pore space with it. Landing costs a third more space than floating did, and that is the whole content of the settled-versus-pending distinction, in one line, derived rather than asserted.

08  Capacity consumed per m³ received

c = fsolids · (1650 / 2650) / (1 − φs) + foil

At the codebase's central cuts — 25 per cent solids, 5.5 per cent oil — and a compacted bed, c = 0.263, falling to about 0.21 once the oil blanket is at target and oil leaves as product. Roughly one cubic metre in four or five of what arrives stays; the rest leaves as displaced brine and needs a home of its own. Every headline number on this page is this one line integrated forward.

10  What this model does not do

  1. Pore brine is not inventoried. The codebase's liquid mass corresponds to free brine only, while the bed's bulk volume already contains its pore space. Mirroring that here means every settling event books a small displaced-brine volume that in a real cavern would simply stay in the pores. The running total of that artefact is shown live beside the closure check. It is a seam in the model being bought, not a rounding error, and it is worth naming before a client finds it.
  2. The time step is one day. Pending settlement therefore cannot resolve a clearing time shorter than that, and its floor is whatever one day of intake brings. The number is meaningful below roughly 20 µm and pinned above it. That matters, because the sensitivity runs out at the same place: at 40 loads a day the pending band is 2.5 per cent of settled capacity at d₅₀ = 5 µm, 0.6 per cent at 10 µm, and 0.16 per cent at 20 µm. The settled-versus-pending distinction is real, but on this geometry it is a sub-one-per-cent correction unless the fines are much finer than assumed, and the number that actually decides the cavern's life is the settled bed.
  3. Oil recovery is instantaneous. The blanket is bailed back to its target within the same step it exceeds it, and the void that frees up refills immediately with returning brine. A real bail-out has a rate, a tank, and a schedule. Set the blanket target to the top of its range to see the other extreme, a cavern that never recovers oil, which costs about fifteen per cent of the void over a full life.
  4. Salt creep is absent. The Phase 00 material names it as one of two sinks and marks it “not modelled in this simulation.” It is absent from the codebase too. Nothing on this page closes that gap.
  5. No incidental leaching during disposal. Disposal runs on saturated carrier fluid, so dissolution is set to zero and the void is flat after day one. Real operations leach a little, and the regulator limits how much.
  6. A right cylinder, 60 m across. Real solution-mined caverns are pear-shaped, so the mapping from volume to depth here is indicative. Volumes are exact; the heights and the standoff reading are not.
  7. The clean-brine floor is a policy, not a measurement. Argonne describes the failure mode but gives no threshold. The slider is where a number would go once one exists.
  8. One cavern, in isolation. Pure operates at least four, and the Phase 00 material's third mechanism is that two of them share one balance by design. Nothing here models a transfer.
  9. Loads are simulated. Fixed seed, cuts drawn from the codebase's own distributions. Nothing on this page has touched Pure's operating data.
